Privacy policy

Short version: Sounding doesn’t collect anything. Last updated 12 November 2026.

Sounding is an independent app made by Alexander Forbes-Reed. It isn’t affiliated with or endorsed by SoundCloud.

What Sounding collects

Nothing. There are no Sounding servers, no Sounding accounts, no analytics and no tracking SDKs, and Sounding shows no ads of its own. I don’t get any data about you or how you use the app. The app talks to SoundCloud and Apple, plus two others if you switch them on: lrclib.net for lyrics, and huggingface.co for the Mac’s AI models. Each has its own section below.

Your SoundCloud account

You sign in on SoundCloud’s own page. Sounding keeps that sign-in in the Keychain on your device, and encrypted in your private iCloud so your other devices can use it. It only ever sends it to SoundCloud, to fetch your library and play your music. Likes, comments and follows go straight to SoundCloud as well, and SoundCloud’s privacy policy covers that data. You don’t have to sign in at all if you only want your own music.

Promoted tracks

SoundCloud puts promoted tracks and playlists in your feed, and Sounding shows them with SoundCloud’s “Promoted” label. As SoundCloud’s own apps do, it calls the tracking addresses SoundCloud sends with each one: an impression when it’s been at least half on screen for a second, a play when you play it, and a click when you open a promoted playlist. Each fires once per item, only while the app is open, and carries no sign-in or cookies. I don’t see any of it, and SoundCloud’s privacy policy covers what it does with it.

What stays on your devices

Your library (likes, playlists, albums and the rest) is kept in a database on the device, so it opens without asking SoundCloud every time. Downloads, Music Analysis results (tempo, key and genre), any BPM or key you’ve set yourself, cue points, setlists, what you were last playing and your settings live on the device too. Music Analysis runs on the device. The network activity log (Settings, Network Activity) stays on the device as well: it lists the app’s own requests with sign-ins and keys blanked out, and on iPhone, iPad and Mac you can export it if you want to send it to me. Nothing sends it for you.

If you’re signed in to iCloud, your SoundCloud library, analysis, cues, setlists and settings sync between your own devices through your private iCloud database. I can’t read it. Settings sync can be turned off in Settings. Settings that belong to your SoundCloud account are removed from the device when you sign out.

Your own music

Music folders you add stay where they are. Sounding reads them on the device to play and analyse them. Your Apple TV plays them straight from your other devices over your home network. They only leave your devices if you turn on Upload to iCloud, which is off until you do. Then they go to your private iCloud, so the TV can still play them when the device they live on is out.

Adding an Apple TV, Mac or Apple Watch

When a signed-out Apple TV is on your Wi-Fi, your iPhone, iPad or Mac offers to sign it in. Spotting it sends nothing. Tap Sign In and the TV plays a short sound, then your device sends the sign-in straight to it over your local network, encrypted with a one-time key. It doesn’t go through any server. The sound, like the QR code on the TV, only carries a single-use secret, never your sign-in. The first time a device links to a TV, the TV shows a six-digit code and waits for you to allow it; linking a TV that’s already signed in sends no sign-in at all. Your Apple Watch gets the sign-in from your iPhone over Apple’s own watch connection.

Microphone, camera and local network

On iPhone, iPad and Mac, the microphone listens for your Apple TV’s sign-in chime, and only while that screen is open. On iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Watch it also listens when you tap Identify Song, to name a song playing around you with Shazam. No audio is kept. The camera is only used if you’d rather scan the code on your Apple TV. Local network access is used to find your own devices, Sonos speakers and network MIDI controllers. None of it is stored or shared.

Heart rate and home lights

Both are off until you turn them on. Match My Workout reads your heart rate from Apple Watch through Health, only while it’s on, to set how energetic Auto Mix plays. The reading stays on your devices. When your Apple TV is the Output, your iPhone sends it an energy level (not your heart rate), and it isn’t stored. If you start a workout from your Apple Watch, it’s saved to Health. Home Lighting uses HomeKit to set the lights you choose, and Sounding doesn’t keep anything about your home.

Listening together

SharePlay is Apple’s. When you start one, Apple carries the track, the queue and where it’s up to between everyone on the call, so you stay in sync. Each person plays the track from SoundCloud with their own sign-in, and nobody’s sign-in is shared.

Lyrics

Lyrics are off until you agree to them the first time you open the Lyrics pane. If you do, Sounding sends the title, artist, album (when it has one) and length of the track you’re playing to lrclib.net, a free lyrics database, and for the next track in the queue as well. Nothing else goes: no sign-in, no account, no device ID, no listening history. Results are kept on your device so the same song isn’t looked up twice. You can turn lyrics off in Settings, Playback. Translating lyrics happens on your iPhone, iPad or Mac with Apple’s own translation, which may download the language from Apple the first time.

On-device AI analysis on Mac

Off until you turn it on in Settings. Switching it on downloads the models, about 223 MB, from huggingface.co. Each file is checked against a hash built into the app, and one that doesn’t match is thrown away. Once they’re downloaded, the analysis runs on your Mac, and your music and its results aren’t sent anywhere. Delete Models in the same place removes them.

Visual Intelligence

If you ask Visual Intelligence about a poster or a screen and pick Sounding, it reads the text in the picture on your device and searches your likes and SoundCloud for it. The picture stays on your device. Only the search goes to SoundCloud.

Song identification

Sounding identifies tracks inside long mixes with Apple’s ShazamKit. For a track inside a mix, it makes a fingerprint from the track’s own audio. When you tap Identify Song, it makes one from what your microphone hears, and only while it’s listening. Either way Apple matches the fingerprint, so Apple’s privacy policy covers that part.

Deleting your data

Sign out in Settings to remove the sign-in from your devices. Delete the app to remove everything it stored on that device. To delete Sounding’s iCloud data, go to Settings, Apple Account, iCloud, Manage Storage.
